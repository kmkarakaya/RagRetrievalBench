Local retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) deployments require corpus-specific embedding selection because retrieval effectiveness can vary across language, domain, document composition, and operating constraints. This paper introduces an end-to-end local benchmarking pipeline for fit-for-purpose embedding-model selection. The pipeline follows a fixed-protocol workflow covering corpus preparation, synthetic query generation, relevance-judgment (qrels) construction, multi-model retrieval evaluation, and automated reporting. It reports six retrieval metrics (\texttt{MAP@10}, \texttt{Recall@10}, \texttt{Precision@10}, \texttt{NDCG@10}, \texttt{MRR@10}, \texttt{Success@10}), preserves machine-readable raw results, and generates timestamped visual and tabular reports for dataset-level and aggregate analysis.

We demonstrate the pipeline on a frozen snapshot with three datasets (\texttt{education\_eng}, \texttt{education\_tr}, \texttt{law\_tr}) and five sentence-transformer models, covering 60 source documents, 1{,}914 chunks, 150 synthetic queries, and 952 positive relevance judgments. Within this snapshot the pipeline exposes language-conditioned ranking changes---English favors monolingual models while both Turkish corpora favor multilingual ones---together with clear quality--speed tradeoffs, whereas the two Turkish domains (education and law/regulation) behave similarly, so language rather than domain dominates difficulty here. In aggregate the multilingual \texttt{M5} model is the strongest quality-first candidate and \texttt{M1} the lowest-latency candidate, while the best English-corpus model is monolingual. To make the model comparison defensible rather than anecdotal, we report query-level bootstrap confidence intervals instead of tests over three dataset means, and we build the relevance judgments from a model-independent candidate pool, confirming that the ranking is stable under an alternative single-model pool. Because the queries are synthetic and the relevance labels are produced by a large language model judge without human validation, these results should be read as bounded, demonstration-level evidence for the workflow rather than as a universal embedding ranking. Overall, the pipeline supports transparent, uncertainty-aware local model selection by combining retrieval effectiveness, operational cost, consistency diagnostics, and query-level statistical support in one reproducible workflow.
