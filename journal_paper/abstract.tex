Local retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) deployments require corpus-specific embedding selection because retrieval effectiveness can vary across language, domain, and document composition. This paper introduces an end-to-end local benchmarking pipeline designed as a fixed-protocol workflow with five stages: corpus preparation, synthetic query generation, relevance-judgment (qrels) construction, multi-model retrieval evaluation, and automated reporting. The pipeline is implemented as a configurable and reproducible software process that reports six retrieval metrics (\texttt{MAP@10}, \texttt{Recall@10}, \texttt{Precision@10}, \texttt{NDCG@10}, \texttt{MRR@10}, \texttt{Success@10}) and preserves both machine-readable raw results and timestamped visual reports.

Its practical advantage is that practitioners can compare candidate models under one shared setup, inspect quality-speed tradeoffs, and reuse artifacts for verification and cross-dataset analysis. We demonstrate the pipeline on a frozen snapshot with three datasets (\texttt{education\_eng}, \texttt{education\_tr}, \texttt{law\_tr}) and five sentence-transformer models, covering 60 source documents, 1{,}914 chunks, 150 synthetic queries, and 624 relevance judgments (qrels). The sample application shows language-sensitive ranking changes, domain-sensitive performance shifts within Turkish corpora, and clear quality-speed tradeoffs, supporting fit-for-purpose local model selection rather than universal superiority claims.
