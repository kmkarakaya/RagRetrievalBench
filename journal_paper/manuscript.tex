\title{Corpus-Specific Embedding-Model Selection for Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Local Benchmarking Pipeline with Query-Level Uncertainty}

\author[1]{Murat Karakaya}
\affil[1]{Department of Software Engineering, TED University, Ankara, T\"urkiye}
\corrauthor[1]{Murat Karakaya}{murat.karakaya@tedu.edu.tr}

% \keywords{Local RAG benchmarking, Embedding-model selection, Corpus-specific evaluation, Retrieval evaluation, Synthetic queries, Relevance judgments}

\begin{abstract}
Practitioners who build retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems often possess the document corpus they want to use, but not the queries and relevance judgments required to benchmark candidate embedding models on that corpus. Without such resources, model choice falls back on public leaderboards or ad hoc trials, although an embedding model that suits one collection should not be transferred automatically to another. This paper presents a configurable local benchmarking pipeline that starts from such an unlabeled local corpus and constructs the benchmark resources needed for corpus-specific embedding-model selection. Each corpus is processed separately through document preparation, synthetic query generation, relevance-judgment construction, multi-model retrieval evaluation, and automated report generation. Each dataset-level report combines retrieval metrics, query-level bootstrap intervals, embedding/insertion throughput, query latency, and preserved artifacts; aggregate reporting is used only as secondary cross-corpus context.

The pipeline is demonstrated through one frozen experimental instantiation on three corpora (English education, Turkish education, and Turkish law/regulation) with five sentence-transformer embedding candidates, 60 source documents, 1{,}914 chunks, 150 generated synthetic queries, and 9{,}296 judged query--chunk pairs, including 952 positive relevance judgments. The purpose of the demonstration is not to make these particular model rankings the main contribution, but to show that the same controlled workflow can expose corpus- and priority-dependent differences. The English corpus produced a different shortlist from the two Turkish corpora, where multilingual candidates led on retrieval quality. The query-level intervals, computed over the 148 scored queries, also showed that the quality gap between the two leading English candidates was not resolved, making operational cost the differentiating factor in that corpus. All evidence remains conditional on the reported snapshot: synthetic queries, unvalidated LLM-generated relevance judgments, a fixed retrieval geometry, a five-model roster, and single-run timing.
\end{abstract}
