\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

This paper is a benchmark-\emph{method} contribution, so we organize prior work around four design questions that determine whether a local embedding benchmark yields trustworthy model-selection evidence: how retrieval is evaluated in practitioner settings, how synthetic queries and relevance judgments are constructed and judged, how embedding rankings shift across language and domain, and how reproducibility and benchmark governance are controlled. We then state the gaps these threads leave open for the present study.

\subsection{Local, Practitioner-Facing RAG Retrieval Evaluation}\label{rw-local-rag}

RAG systems depend on retrieval quality to maintain answer grounding and downstream reliability, so retrieval design remains a central determinant of end-task behavior. Foundational studies show that weaker retrieval can directly reduce grounding quality, and survey evidence frames IR--LLM integration as a multi-component pipeline spanning query rewriting, retrieval, reranking, and reader/generator interaction, while highlighting hallucination risk under weak grounding. \citep{Lewis_RAG_2021, Zhu_LLMIR_Survey_2025} Applied studies reinforce this in domain-specific settings: knowledge-graph-augmented RAG for power-network cybersecurity degrades when similarity-retrieval modules are removed, customs-regulation KG-RAG improves answer accuracy and relevance, and QuIM-RAG shows retrieval-oriented inverted question matching improving question-answering effectiveness over conventional baselines. \citep{Gao_KG_RAG_Power_2025, Hu_ICCARAG_2025, Saha_QuIMRAG_2024} Metadata-aware retrieval likewise improves ranking quality in practical question-answering environments. \citep{Ghasemi_Metadata_QR_2024}

Pipeline-focused studies on local and enterprise collections move closer to deployment-oriented evaluation but remain heterogeneous in objectives and scoring: a closed-domain dementia-care workflow emphasizing cross-domain replication, a statistical evaluation pipeline for industrial semantic search that reports semantic-plus-lexical hybrid retrieval as mitigation for specialized terminology, a telecom hybrid-embedding architecture improving domain question-answering and code retrieval, domain-adaptive fine-tuning for engineering retrieval under constrained resources, and a systematic review noting recurring resource and runtime costs. \citep{Cummings_2025, Yozkan_2025, Khan_TelcoGPT_2025, Szabo_2025, Steve_2023} These works optimize one application pipeline rather than supporting a shared-protocol comparison of a candidate model roster on a practitioner's own corpus.

\subsection{Synthetic Queries, LLM-as-Judge, and Qrels Construction}\label{rw-qrels}

Because fully curated relevance labels are expensive, recent work uses language models to generate queries and to judge relevance. Agentic and multimodal RAG settings embed an LLM-as-a-judge that critiques responses across quality and safety dimensions, and enterprise deployments report LLM judging as generally reliable but an imperfect substitute for human assessment; multilingual judge calibration is itself non-trivial, with calibrated, uncertainty-aware scoring improving human alignment. \citep{Bellard_2025, Wang_Birman_2025, Kunuku_2025, Taemkaeo_2026, Wen_2026} Evaluation-design studies make the reliability question explicit by comparing LLM and human judgments under controlled protocols: agreement is substantial but condition-dependent, and judge configuration should be treated as a measurable design component rather than a fixed shortcut. \citep{thakur2025support, abdallah2025rerankarena, miyaji2025evaluating} Work on synthetic-data construction adds concrete quality controls relevant to qrels design---judge validation with executability checks and automatic repair, targeted query synthesis for structured reasoning, and synthetic query labeling competitive with real-data training. \citep{caferouglu2025sing, huang-etal-2025-targa, Bassani_2021}

A specific and under-discussed design choice within this thread is how the \emph{candidate pool} that a judge labels is constructed. In classical information-retrieval benchmark construction, relevance judgments are pooled from the outputs of multiple retrieval systems so that the resulting ground truth does not privilege any single retriever; the RAG-evaluation studies above similarly treat judgment collection across retrieval stages and judge configuration as first-class design variables \citep{thakur2025support, abdallah2025rerankarena, miyaji2025evaluating}. When a benchmark instead draws its candidate pool from one retriever that is also under evaluation, relevant passages that only other systems surface can be missed and silently counted as non-relevant, biasing the comparison toward the pool-generating model. The present paper adopts model-independent pooling for exactly this reason and, in Section~\ref{results}, quantifies how much the model ranking depends on this choice.

\subsection{Cross-Language and Cross-Domain Embedding Selection}\label{rw-shift}

Benchmark-oriented studies show that embedding rankings depend on corpus characteristics and language context: corpus type and query format explain retrieval variance at a level comparable to model choice and shift rankings across corpora, and domain-specific question-answering rankings diverge substantially from public benchmarks with practical efficiency tradeoffs. \citep{Mikkelsen_2026, Margit_2025} Controlled local-corpus studies add complementary evidence---an enterprise benchmark grounded to a released knowledge-base snapshot, domain-adaptive embeddings with distillation-based reranking, cross-model embedding transformation to reduce migration overhead, and representation analyses showing corpus-dependent similarity saturation. \citep{cohen2025wixqa, luo2025domain, yoon2025embedding, dvovravckova2025analyzing} Domain- and language-specialized studies reinforce that protocols tuned for one setting do not transfer automatically: task-aligned embeddings help environmental question-answering, and multilingual, Turkish-focused (TR-MTEB), and Persian benchmarks show ranking behavior conditioned on similarity formulation and corpus composition, alongside an open-source embedding survey weighing dimensionality, latency, and memory tradeoffs. \citep{Sajja_2025, Song_2024, Baysan_2025, Kalantari_2024, Abimbola_2026} Further multilingual studies make transfer sensitivity explicit through translation-based embedding, two-phase cross-lingual retrieval, language-specific models, and evidence that cross-lingual transfer can still degrade for low-resource settings. \citep{Iscan_2024, nezhad2025cross, mekonnen2025optimized, Gutu_2025, Iana_2026} This body of work establishes that embedding selection is context-sensitive but rarely treats cross-language and cross-domain comparability of one shared roster, under one protocol, as a first-class objective.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Benchmark Governance}\label{rw-repro}

A final thread emphasizes that evaluation quality depends on operational controls, not only model choice. ReproRAG quantifies retrieval reproducibility under changes in embedding models, precision, retrieval algorithms, and hardware; an end-to-end storage-aware framework profiles system-level benchmarking; RAGBench and the TRACe framework support explainable large-scale evaluation while reporting that LLM-based evaluators can underperform a fine-tuned RoBERTa evaluator; and RAGOps and software-traceability studies extend the lifecycle and indexing-choice perspective. \citep{wang2025reproducibility, xu2025end, friel2024ragbench, xu2025ragops, ali2024establishing} These works motivate reporting explicit reproducibility conditions and preserving raw artifacts, yet reusable artifacts for repeatable \emph{local} evaluation across document collections are seldom the primary contribution.

\subsection{Gap Statement and Positioning}\label{rw-gap}

Across these threads, four gaps are central to this paper. Most prior work optimizes a single application pipeline rather than supporting practitioner-oriented local model selection under one shared protocol (\textbf{RQ1}); cross-language and cross-domain comparability of the same roster is rarely a first-class objective (\textbf{RQ2}, \textbf{RQ3}); quality--speed tradeoff evidence for deployment is reported less often than answer-quality gains (\textbf{RQ4}); and reusable benchmarking artifacts that make local evaluation repeatable are seldom the deliverable. The candidate-pool construction issue noted above is a further, method-level gap that this paper treats explicitly. In response, this paper contributes a reusable, practitioner-facing pipeline that evaluates one shared embedding roster under a fixed protocol across local datasets, constructs relevance judgments with model-independent pooling, and reports retrieval quality, operational cost, and query-level uncertainty as consolidated artifacts. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

\subsection{From Preliminary Conference Evidence to the Current Journal Study}\label{preliminary-conference-study-short-context}

Our earlier conference paper established proof-of-feasibility for a local automated benchmarking workflow on a single technical English corpus, including synthetic-query generation, LLM-based qrels construction, and multi-model retrieval scoring. That baseline was intentionally narrow in evidential scope: it did not test cross-language behavior, did not test cross-domain behavior within a shared language, and did not support cross-dataset consistency claims under one fixed protocol. \citep{Karakaya_Conference_2025} The current journal paper addresses those coverage limits rather than repeating the feasibility baseline, extending it to controlled cross-dataset comparison under a consistent evaluation frame with explicit source-provenance documentation. \citep{TED_Regulations, BDDK_Mevzuat}\label{extension-in-the-current-journal-work} Methodologically, the progression from conference to journal scope is both procedural and evidential: from demonstrating pipeline feasibility in one corpus to a controlled comparative case study across language and domain conditions that matter for practitioner-facing local deployment decisions. Relative to the conference baseline, the journal study adds aggregate cross-dataset ranking, consistency-oriented comparison, model-independent qrels pooling, and explicit quality--speed tradeoff evidence, together with reusable reporting artifacts. \citep{Karakaya_Conference_2025, GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}
