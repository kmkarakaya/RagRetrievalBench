\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

This paper is a practitioner-facing benchmark-\emph{method} contribution, so we organize prior work around four design questions that determine whether a local embedding benchmark can support corpus-specific model shortlisting: how retrieval is evaluated in public and local settings, how synthetic queries and relevance judgments are constructed and judged, how embedding rankings vary with language- and domain-associated corpus characteristics, and how reproducibility and benchmark governance are controlled. We then state the gaps these threads leave open for the present study.

\subsection{Local, Practitioner-Facing RAG Retrieval Evaluation}\label{rw-local-rag}

RAG systems depend on retrieval quality to maintain answer grounding and downstream reliability, so retrieval design remains a central determinant of end-task behavior. Foundational studies show that weaker retrieval can directly reduce grounding quality, and survey evidence frames IR--LLM integration as a multi-component pipeline spanning query rewriting, retrieval, reranking, and reader/generator interaction, while highlighting hallucination risk under weak grounding. \citep{Lewis_RAG_2021, Zhu_LLMIR_Survey_2025} Public benchmark suites such as BEIR and MTEB provide broad shared-testbed evidence for retrieval and embedding models, but their strength as reusable leaderboards also limits their ability to answer a practitioner's local corpus question directly: whether the same candidate roster should be selected differently for separate corpora under the practitioner's control. \citep{Thakur_BEIR_2021, Muennighoff_MTEB_2023}

Pipeline-focused studies on local and enterprise collections move closer to deployment-oriented evaluation but remain heterogeneous in objectives and scoring. Examples include closed-domain health-information workflows, industrial semantic search, telecom retrieval architectures, domain-adaptive engineering retrieval, and reviews that emphasize resource and runtime costs. \citep{Cummings_2025, Yozkan_2025, Khan_TelcoGPT_2025, Szabo_2025, Steve_2023} These works are useful evidence that local context matters, but they usually optimize one application pipeline rather than offering a repeatable shared-protocol comparison of a candidate embedding roster across the practitioner's own corpora.

\subsection{Synthetic Queries, LLM-as-Judge, and Qrels Construction}\label{rw-qrels}

Because fully curated relevance labels are expensive, recent work uses language models to generate queries and to judge relevance. Agentic and multimodal RAG settings embed an LLM-as-a-judge that critiques responses across quality and safety dimensions, and enterprise deployments report LLM judging as useful but still an imperfect substitute for human assessment; multilingual judge calibration is itself non-trivial, with calibrated, uncertainty-aware scoring improving human alignment. \citep{Bellard_2025, Wang_Birman_2025, Kunuku_2025, Taemkaeo_2026, Wen_2026} Evaluation-design studies make the reliability question explicit by comparing LLM and human judgments under controlled protocols: agreement is substantial but condition-dependent, and judge configuration should be treated as a measurable design component rather than a fixed shortcut. \citep{thakur2025support, abdallah2025rerankarena, miyaji2025evaluating} Work on synthetic-data construction adds concrete quality controls relevant to qrels design---judge validation with executability checks and automatic repair, targeted query synthesis for structured reasoning, and synthetic query labeling competitive with real-data training. \citep{caferouglu2025sing, huang-etal-2025-targa, Bassani_2021}

A specific design choice within this thread is how the \emph{candidate pool} that a judge labels is constructed. In TREC-style information-retrieval evaluation, relevance judgments are commonly pooled from the outputs of multiple retrieval systems so that the judged set does not privilege any single retriever by construction. \citep{Voorhees_Evaluation_2002} The RAG-evaluation studies above similarly treat judgment collection across retrieval stages and judge configuration as first-class design variables. \citep{thakur2025support, abdallah2025rerankarena, miyaji2025evaluating} When a benchmark instead draws its candidate pool from one retriever that is also under evaluation, relevant passages that only other systems surface can be missed and silently counted as non-relevant, biasing the comparison toward the pool-generating model. The present paper adopts model-independent pooling within the evaluated roster for this reason and, in Section~\ref{results}, reports a bounded comparison against an earlier single-model pool.

\subsection{Cross-Language and Cross-Domain Embedding Selection}\label{rw-shift}

Benchmark-oriented studies show that embedding rankings can depend on corpus characteristics and language context: corpus type and query format explain retrieval variance at a level comparable to model choice and shift rankings across corpora, and domain-specific question-answering rankings can diverge from public benchmarks while exposing practical efficiency tradeoffs. \citep{Mikkelsen_2026, Margit_2025} Controlled local-corpus studies add complementary evidence from enterprise retrieval, domain-adaptive embeddings, embedding migration, and corpus-dependent similarity analyses. \citep{cohen2025wixqa, luo2025domain, yoon2025embedding, dvovravckova2025analyzing} Domain- and language-specialized studies reinforce that protocols tuned for one setting do not transfer automatically: task-aligned embeddings help environmental question-answering, and multilingual, Turkish-focused (TR-MTEB), and Persian benchmarks show ranking behavior conditioned on similarity formulation and corpus composition, alongside an open-source embedding survey weighing dimensionality, latency, and memory tradeoffs. \citep{Sajja_2025, Song_2024, Baysan_2025, Kalantari_2024, Abimbola_2026} Further multilingual studies make transfer sensitivity explicit through translation-based embedding, two-phase cross-lingual retrieval, language-specific models, and evidence that cross-lingual transfer can still degrade for low-resource settings. \citep{Iscan_2024, nezhad2025cross, mekonnen2025optimized, Gutu_2025, Iana_2026} This body of work supports the practical premise that language and domain should guide corpus partitioning and interpretation, but it does not remove the need to benchmark each local corpus under the same candidate roster and protocol.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Benchmark Governance}\label{rw-repro}

A final thread emphasizes that evaluation quality depends on operational controls, not only model choice. ReproRAG quantifies retrieval reproducibility under changes in embedding models, precision, retrieval algorithms, and hardware; an end-to-end storage-aware framework profiles system-level benchmarking; RAGBench and the TRACe framework support explainable large-scale evaluation while reporting that LLM-based evaluators can underperform a fine-tuned RoBERTa evaluator; and RAGOps and software-traceability studies extend the lifecycle and indexing-choice perspective. \citep{wang2025reproducibility, xu2025end, friel2024ragbench, xu2025ragops, ali2024establishing} These works motivate explicit reproducibility conditions and preserved raw artifacts, yet reusable artifacts for repeatable \emph{local} per-corpus embedding selection are seldom the primary deliverable.

\subsection{Gap Statement and Positioning}\label{rw-gap}

Across these threads, four gaps are central to this paper. Public benchmarks such as BEIR and MTEB are essential shared references, but they do not by themselves build a benchmark from a practitioner's own corpus. Many local RAG studies optimize one application pipeline rather than supporting practitioner-oriented per-corpus model shortlisting under one shared protocol (\textbf{RQ1}); language- and domain-associated corpus comparisons of the same evaluated roster are less often treated as local reporting outputs (\textbf{RQ2}, \textbf{RQ3}); descriptive quality--speed tradeoffs are reported less often than answer-quality gains (\textbf{RQ4}); and reusable benchmark artifacts that make local evaluation inspectable and repeatable are seldom the deliverable. The candidate-pool construction issue noted above is a further method-level gap that this paper treats explicitly. In response, this paper contributes a reusable, practitioner-facing pipeline that evaluates one bounded embedding roster under a fixed protocol separately for each local corpus, constructs relevance judgments with model-independent pooling within that roster, and reports retrieval quality, operational cost, and query-level uncertainty as corpus-specific artifacts. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

A preliminary version of this line of work demonstrated the feasibility of automated local embedding-model benchmarking on a single English technical corpus, using synthetic-query generation, LLM-assisted relevance construction, and multi-model retrieval scoring. \citep{Karakaya_Conference_2025} The present paper shifts the contribution from one-corpus feasibility to practitioner-facing, corpus-specific model selection. It evaluates the same bounded candidate roster separately across three language- and domain-differentiated local corpora with explicit source-provenance boundaries, treats dataset-level reports as the primary selection artifact, adds model-independent pooled qrels within the evaluated roster, reports query-level descriptive uncertainty, and incorporates retrieval-quality and timing evidence for corpus-specific shortlisting. \citep{TED_Regulations, BDDK_Mevzuat, GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}
