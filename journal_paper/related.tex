\section{Related Work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{RAG Retrieval Evaluation and Embedding Benchmarking}\label{rag-retrieval-evaluation-and-embedding-benchmarking}

RAG systems depend on retrieval quality to maintain answer grounding and downstream reliability, so retrieval design remains a central determinant of end-task behavior. Foundational work establishes this dependency, and later survey evidence frames IR--LLM integration as a multi-component pipeline spanning query rewriting, retrieval, reranking, and reader/generator interaction, while also highlighting failure modes such as hallucination risk under weak grounding. \citep{Lewis_RAG_2021, Zhu_LLMIR_Survey_2025}

Recent applied studies reinforce this point in domain-specific settings. Gao and Xing design a knowledge-graph-augmented RAG framework for cybersecurity vulnerabilities in power networks and report that removing similarity-retrieval and problem-solving modules degrades response relevance and accuracy. Hu et al. integrate customs-regulation knowledge graphs with RAG and report improvements in answer accuracy, relevance, and contextual accuracy. \citep{Gao_KG_RAG_Power_2025, Hu_ICCARAG_2025} QuIM-RAG further demonstrates that retrieval-oriented inverted question matching can improve question-answering effectiveness over conventional RAG baselines under BERTScore and RAGAS evaluation. \citep{Saha_QuIMRAG_2024}

Related evidence from question-retrieval research also indicates that metadata-aware retrieval strategies can improve ranking quality in practical question-answering environments; Ghasemi and Shakery report strong gains with transformer-based metadata integration and RAG-oriented query expansion. \citep{Ghasemi_Metadata_QR_2024}

Pipeline-focused studies on local and enterprise document collections move closer to deployment-oriented evaluation, but they remain heterogeneous in objectives and scoring design. Cummings et al. present a closed-domain dementia-care RAG workflow that combines data extraction, question-answer generation, and fine-tuning, with explicit emphasis on replication across domains. Yozkan and Supriyanto propose a statistical evaluation pipeline for industrial semantic search and report precision gains when document volume increases, while also noting risks from excessive specialized terminology and mitigation via semantic-plus-lexical hybrid retrieval. Khan et al. report that a telecom-specific hybrid-embedding architecture and document-processing pipeline improves both domain Q\&A and code retrieval relative to single-embedding baselines. Szabo et al. similarly report improved engineering retrieval from domain-adaptive fine-tuning of dense retrieval models under constrained training resources. Complementing these implementation studies, Steve's systematic review of domain-specific contextualized embeddings highlights recurring resource and runtime costs across domains. \citep{Cummings_2025, Yozkan_2025, Khan_TelcoGPT_2025, Szabo_2025, Steve_2023}

Synthetic-query and judge-construction studies add a complementary perspective for evaluation design when fully curated relevance labels are expensive. Bellard et al. evaluate an agentic heart-failure RAG setting on synthetic QA pairs, where an embedded LLM-as-a-Judge critiques responses across multiple quality and safety dimensions in a feedback loop. Wang and Birman similarly report using LLM-as-judge reasoning to explain conclusions in multimodal cloud-instability diagnosis. Kunuku and Dehbozorgi apply a two-stage evaluation process that combines retrieval-generation metrics with agreement analysis between LLM judges and human raters, and they report divergence on deeper reasoning and visual interpretation tasks. Taemkaeo et al. also use LLM-as-judge for large-scale enterprise HR answer evaluation in Thai, describing it as generally reliable but imperfect as a substitute for human assessment. Wen et al. further show that multilingual judge calibration is non-trivial and report improved human-alignment and reduced fairness gaps through calibrated, uncertainty-aware scoring. \citep{Bellard_2025, Wang_Birman_2025, Kunuku_2025, Taemkaeo_2026, Wen_2026}

Evidence from synthetic data construction also highlights concrete quality-control policies that are directly relevant to qrels design. Caferoglu et al. describe a quality-aware synthesis pipeline that combines LLM-judge validation with executability checks and automatic repair, while Huang et al. propose targeted query synthesis for structured reasoning tasks and report improved sample efficiency and non-I.I.D. generalization without manual annotation. Bassani and Pasi similarly show that synthetic query generation can support semantic query labeling with performance comparable to models trained on real-world data, alongside dataset release for reproducible evaluation. These findings support the paper's emphasis on controlled synthetic-query and relevance-signal construction as first-class evaluation-design choices rather than auxiliary implementation details. \citep{caferouglu2025sing, huang-etal-2025-targa, Bassani_2021}

Reproducibility and benchmark-governance studies further emphasize that evaluation quality depends on operational controls, not only model choice. Wang et al. introduce ReproRAG to quantify retrieval reproducibility under changes in embedding models, precision, retrieval algorithms, hardware, and distributed execution, explicitly framing reproducibility-performance tradeoffs. Xu's end-to-end RASB framework similarly targets system-level benchmarking with workload generation and hardware-aware profiling to expose storage-compute interactions. Friel et al. contribute RAGBench and the TRACe evaluation framework to support explainable large-scale RAG evaluation, while also reporting that LLM-based RAG evaluators can underperform a fine-tuned RoBERTa evaluator. Xu et al. extend this lifecycle perspective through RAGOps considerations for operating and managing continuously evolving RAG pipelines. From a complementary software traceability setting, Ali et al. compare keyword, vector, and graph indexing combinations in an LLM-supported RAG setup and report practical gains over conventional baselines. \citep{wang2025reproducibility, xu2025end, friel2024ragbench, xu2025ragops, ali2024establishing}

Taken together, these studies establish that retrieval design, pipeline construction, judge-construction choices, and reproducibility governance materially affect downstream QA quality, but they leave four gaps that are central to this paper's claims. First, most prior studies optimize one application pipeline rather than supporting practitioner-oriented local model selection under a common benchmark protocol. Second, cross-language and cross-domain comparability of the same embedding roster is rarely treated as a first-class evaluation objective. Third, prior work emphasizes answer quality improvements more than explicit quality-speed tradeoff analysis for deployment decisions. Fourth, reusable benchmarking artifacts that enable repeatable local evaluation across document collections are not usually the primary contribution. In contrast, this paper focuses on controlled cross-dataset embedding-model selection under one fixed protocol and public reporting workflow for practitioner-facing local deployment decisions. \citep{Gao_KG_RAG_Power_2025, Hu_ICCARAG_2025, Saha_QuIMRAG_2024, Ghasemi_Metadata_QR_2024, Cummings_2025, Yozkan_2025, Khan_TelcoGPT_2025, Szabo_2025, Steve_2023, Bellard_2025, Wang_Birman_2025, Kunuku_2025, Taemkaeo_2026, Wen_2026, wang2025reproducibility, xu2025end, friel2024ragbench, xu2025ragops, ali2024establishing, GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

\subsection{Embedding Selection Under Corpus, Domain, and Language Shift}\label{embedding-selection-under-corpus-domain-and-language-shift}

Recent benchmark-oriented studies provide direct evidence that embedding-model rankings depend on corpus characteristics and language context. Mikkelsen reports that corpus type, query format, and their interactions explain retrieval-performance variance at a level comparable to model choice in clinical RAG settings, and that model rankings shift across corpora. Margit similarly reports substantial ranking variation between domain-specific Hungarian QA retrieval and a public benchmark, together with practical efficiency tradeoffs. \citep{Mikkelsen_2026, Margit_2025}

Local-corpus model-selection studies under controlled evaluation settings add complementary evidence for fixed-protocol decisions. Cohen et al. introduce WixQA as a multi-dataset enterprise RAG benchmark with question-answer sets explicitly grounded to a released knowledge-base snapshot, enabling end-to-end retrieval-generation evaluation in one consistent corpus context. Luo et al. report that combining domain-adaptive embeddings with knowledge-distillation-based reranking improves performance on both a private power-domain dataset and a public benchmark, with ablation results supporting the contribution of each optimization component. Yoon and Arik address model-migration overhead by proposing embedding transformation across models, reporting substantial speed and cost reductions that make iterative local evaluation more operationally feasible. At the representation-analysis level, Dvoravckova shows that embedding similarity behavior depends strongly on co-occurrence and contextual statistics and can exhibit saturation effects, reinforcing the need for corpus-aware interpretation of model behavior. \citep{cohen2025wixqa, luo2025domain, yoon2025embedding, dvovravckova2025analyzing}

Domain-specialized modeling studies reinforce this pattern from another direction. Sajja et al. show that task- and domain-aligned embedding models can improve semantic retrieval quality in hydrology and environmental QA, especially in formats that require stronger semantic precision. In parallel, multilingual benchmark research indicates that evaluation protocols designed for one language setting are not automatically portable to others; this includes general multilingual benchmark framing and Turkish-focused sentence-embedding benchmarking through TR-MTEB. Persian-language evidence points to the same conclusion: Kalantari et al. evaluate 17 embedding models across four QA datasets and report that model ranking behavior depends on retrieval similarity formulation and corpus composition, further highlighting language-conditioned selection effects. \citep{Sajja_2025, Song_2024, Baysan_2025, Kalantari_2024} Abimbola et al. complement these findings with a systematic open-source embedding survey spanning literature through 2025, comparing operational tradeoffs (for example, dimensionality, latency, and memory use) and discussing benchmark limitations such as bias and robustness concerns, which further supports context-aware local model selection over static global ranking assumptions. \citep{Abimbola_2026}

Additional multilingual comparability studies make fairness and transfer sensitivity more explicit. Iscan et al. report that translation-based English embedding can improve non-English RAG context precision and recall relative to direct multilingual embedding. Nezhad and Agrawal show that a two-phase cross-lingual retrieval design improves long-context retrieval behavior across seven languages and 49 language pairs while reducing mid-context failure effects. Mekonnen et al. report that Amharic-specific embedding models improve passage-retrieval metrics over multilingual baselines, and Gutu and Popescu similarly report cross-lingual MRR gains from bilingual corpus construction with fine-tuned embeddings, while noting remaining imbalance challenges in tabular retrieval. Outside RAG, Iana et al. provide convergent benchmark evidence that cross-lingual transfer quality can still decline, especially for low-resource settings, even under multilingual model families. \citep{Iscan_2024, nezhad2025cross, mekonnen2025optimized, Gutu_2025, Iana_2026}

Taken together, these studies support the claim that embedding selection is context-sensitive, but they still leave a practical integration gap for this paper's objective: a reusable, practitioner-facing workflow that evaluates one shared embedding roster under one fixed protocol across local datasets while reporting both retrieval quality and operational behavior for deployment decisions. This paper addresses that gap through controlled cross-dataset benchmarking and consolidated reporting artifacts. \citep{Mikkelsen_2026, Margit_2025, cohen2025wixqa, luo2025domain, yoon2025embedding, dvovravckova2025analyzing, Iscan_2024, nezhad2025cross, mekonnen2025optimized, Gutu_2025, Iana_2026, GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

\subsection{Preliminary Conference Study (Short Context)}\label{preliminary-conference-study-short-context}

Our earlier conference paper (\texttt{publishedConferencePaper.pdf}) introduced a local automated benchmark pipeline and demonstrated feasibility on a single technical English corpus. The conference version established end-to-end evaluation with synthetic queries, LLM-based qrels, and multi-model retrieval scoring, but it did not yet provide cross-dataset comparative evidence for language and domain sensitivity. \citep{Karakaya_Conference_2025}

\subsection{Extension in the Current Journal Work}\label{extension-in-the-current-journal-work}

This journal paper extends that baseline in scope and claim coverage. It uses a frozen cross-dataset setup with \texttt{education\_eng}, \texttt{education\_tr}, and \texttt{law\_tr}, evaluates five embedding models under one consistent pipeline configuration, and documents source provenance explicitly. \citep{TED_Regulations, BDDK_Mevzuat} The extension then adds aggregate cross-dataset ranking, consistency analysis, and explicit quality-speed tradeoff analysis, together with automated reporting artifacts designed for reuse. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

In claim--gap terms, prior application-focused RAG studies justify why retrieval benchmarking matters, while the current paper addresses how practitioners can perform transparent, repeatable, and corpus-specific embedding-model selection under a fixed evaluation frame. \citep{Karakaya_Conference_2025, GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}
