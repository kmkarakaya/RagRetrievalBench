\section{Results}\label{results}

This section reports the evidence produced by the proposed benchmarking pipeline under the fixed experimental setup described in Section~\ref{experiments}. The purpose of the reported results is practical model selection for local corpora rather than universal leaderboard comparison. Because the pipeline generates full benchmark reports automatically and dynamically, only a selected subset of the most decision-relevant tables and figures is included here. The full automatically generated reports and supporting artifacts are available through the public repository for interested readers. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench, Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Eng_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Tr_2026_03_05, Raw_Law_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Headline Findings}\label{cross-dataset-headline-findings}

The fixed three-dataset snapshot yields three immediate findings. First, the multilingual mpnet model (\texttt{M5}) is the strongest overall candidate by average rank and mean \texttt{MAP@10}. Second, retrieval difficulty is dataset-dependent: \texttt{education\_eng} is the easiest benchmark in this paper, whereas \texttt{law\_tr} is the hardest. Third, the evidence already suggests both a language effect and a quality-speed tradeoff, which are examined in more detail later in this section. Table~\ref{tbl:dataset_result_summary}, Table~\ref{tbl:cross_dataset_consistency_summary}, and Figure~\ref{fig:results_cross_dataset_performance} summarize these headline results. \citep{Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05}

Table~\ref{tbl:education_eng_timing} and Figure~\ref{fig:results_education_eng_model_comparison} together show that the stronger English results are accompanied by a broad range of operational costs, especially between the lightweight MiniLM models and the heavier mpnet variants. \citep{Raw_Education_Eng_2026_03_05}

Table~\ref{tbl:education_tr_timing} and Figure~\ref{fig:results_education_tr_model_comparison} show that this quality advantage is obtained at higher operational cost, especially for the mpnet-based multilingual model. \citep{Raw_Education_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Dataset-Level Results: law\_tr}\label{results-law-tr}

The Turkish law/regulation subset is the most difficult benchmark in the fixed snapshot, yet it preserves the same top-two ordering observed in \texttt{education\_tr}. Table~\ref{tbl:law_tr_metrics} shows that \texttt{M5} remains the best model, followed by \texttt{M3}, while the remaining models trail at substantially lower scores. Compared with \texttt{education\_tr}, however, the absolute performance level decreases for all models, which indicates that domain shift within the same language still materially changes retrieval difficulty. Table~\ref{tbl:law_tr_timing} and Figure~\ref{fig:results_law_tr_model_comparison} show that the operational ranking remains broadly similar to the other datasets: the lighter MiniLM models are faster, whereas the stronger multilingual mpnet model is slower but more effective. \citep{Raw_Law_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Language Effect}\label{language-effect}

The comparison between Table~\ref{tbl:education_eng_metrics} and Table~\ref{tbl:education_tr_metrics} shows a clear language effect within the education domain. In English, the strongest three models are monolingual and tightly clustered near \texttt{MAP@10} of 0.61. In Turkish, the multilingual models become decisively better, with \texttt{M5} and \texttt{M3} substantially outperforming the monolingual alternatives. This shift is also reflected in the automatically generated per-dataset figures shown earlier and is the clearest evidence in the paper that the most suitable embedding model depends on corpus language rather than on one global ranking alone. \citep{Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Eng_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Domain Effect Within Turkish}\label{domain-effect-within-turkish}

The comparison between Table~\ref{tbl:education_tr_metrics} and Table~\ref{tbl:law_tr_metrics} isolates a domain effect under the same language. The ranking order of the strongest two Turkish-capable models remains stable, with \texttt{M5} first and \texttt{M3} second on both datasets. However, the absolute scores decline on the law/regulation corpus: \texttt{M5} decreases from 0.5508 to 0.3659 in \texttt{MAP@10}, and \texttt{M3} decreases from 0.4876 to 0.2948. The domain shift therefore changes the absolute level of retrieval effectiveness even when the identity of the best candidate model remains unchanged. \citep{Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Tr_2026_03_05, Raw_Law_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Quality-Speed Tradeoff}\label{quality-speed-tradeoff}

Table~\ref{tbl:quality_speed_summary} combines aggregate quality and timing evidence. The strongest overall model, \texttt{M5}, has the highest mean \texttt{MAP@10} and the best average rank, but it is substantially slower than the MiniLM variants in both embedding throughput and query latency. Conversely, \texttt{M1} is the fastest model in the benchmark, yet it trails the strongest multilingual models in aggregate retrieval quality. This contrast is the most practically important operational finding of the paper: the best quality model is not the fastest model, and the preferred choice therefore depends on whether the practitioner prioritizes retrieval effectiveness, indexing cost, or interactive latency. \citep{Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Eng_2026_03_05, Raw_Education_Tr_2026_03_05, Raw_Law_Tr_2026_03_05}

\subsection{Statistical Note}\label{statistical-note}

The aggregate report indicates that the difference between \texttt{M5} and \texttt{M4} on \texttt{MAP@10} is not statistically significant under the report's pairwise testing configuration (\texttt{p=0.339}). The same report also records a non-significant Friedman test result across the five benchmarked models (\texttt{p=0.483}). \citep{Aggregate_Report_2026_03_05} These outputs should be interpreted cautiously because the statistical comparison is based on only three datasets. In the present paper, the statistical evidence therefore serves as supporting context rather than as the primary basis for broad claims about model superiority.

The broader practical value of the results lies in the reporting capability of the proposed pipeline itself. For each dataset and for the aggregate analysis, the system automatically generates substantially richer report content than can be accommodated in the paper, including additional metric breakdowns, timing analyses, and visual summaries. The paper therefore presents a selected subset of the most decision-relevant evidence, while interested readers can consult the public repository for the full automatically generated reports and supporting materials. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}
