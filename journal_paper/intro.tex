\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems depend on retrieval quality to ground generation, so embedding-model choice directly shapes downstream answer reliability. In operational settings, this choice is rarely made on generic corpora; it is made on local document collections with domain-specific language, structure, and constraints. \citep{Lewis_RAG_2021}

Public leaderboards remain informative as external references, but they are not sufficient for deployment decisions on local corpora. A model that performs strongly in one benchmark context may not remain optimal when corpus language, domain vocabulary, or document composition changes. This gap motivates local, controlled, and repeatable benchmarking procedures that produce corpus-specific evidence for practitioner decision making.

\subsection{Motivation}\label{motivation}

A practical evaluation workflow must satisfy two requirements simultaneously. First, candidate embedding models must be compared under one shared protocol so that observed differences are attributable to model behavior rather than to inconsistent evaluation settings. Second, the workflow must produce reusable outputs that support both immediate selection decisions and later re-analysis.

These requirements motivate an end-to-end local benchmarking pipeline that materializes benchmark artifacts, evaluates multiple models consistently, and reports both retrieval effectiveness and operational behavior in structured outputs. This design targets fit-for-purpose selection for practitioners rather than universal ranking claims. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

\subsection{Problem Framing and Paper Objective}\label{study-setup}

This paper addresses the following problem: how can practitioners select embedding models for local RAG deployment using transparent and reproducible evidence rather than ad hoc trial-and-error? To answer this problem, the paper presents a reusable local benchmarking pipeline that supports controlled multi-model evaluation and automatic report generation from one consistent workflow. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}

The workflow is demonstrated on a fixed experimental snapshot covering three datasets and five embedding models. Setup details, including provenance and controlled parameters, are provided in Section~\ref{experiments}; quantitative comparative evidence is reported in Section~\ref{results}.

\subsection{Contributions and Research Questions}\label{contributions}

The paper makes four contributions to local RAG benchmarking practice:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  It defines a reusable pipeline for corpus-specific embedding-model selection under a controlled evaluation frame.
\item
  It integrates automatic report generation that consolidates retrieval quality and operational measurements into decision-support artifacts.
\item
  It supports cross-dataset comparability through shared protocol execution and aggregate reporting.
\item
  It provides a publicly accessible implementation and benchmark artifacts to improve reuse and reproducibility in practitioner workflows. \citep{GitHub_RagRetrievalBench}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Research Questions}\label{research-questions}
Guided by these contributions, the empirical analysis addresses four research questions:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  RQ1 (Pipeline Utility): Can the proposed pipeline produce actionable evidence for local embedding-model selection?
\item
  RQ2 (Language Sensitivity): Under fixed settings, how does model behavior vary between English and Turkish education corpora?
\item
  RQ3 (Domain Sensitivity): Within Turkish corpora, how does model behavior vary between education and law/regulation datasets?
\item
  RQ4 (Operational Tradeoff): How does the pipeline expose quality-speed tradeoffs for fit-for-purpose deployment decisions?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope and Paper Organization}\label{study-boundaries}

The claims are bounded to the fixed snapshot and evaluated corpus set. The paper does not claim universal model superiority beyond this scope; instead, it evaluates whether the proposed workflow produces reliable local evidence for fit-for-purpose selection under controlled conditions.

Section~\ref{related-work} positions the paper against prior work, Section~\ref{method} describes the proposed workflow, Section~\ref{experiments} documents the experimental setup, Section~\ref{results} reports comparative evidence, and Section~\ref{conclusion} concludes with implications and future directions.
